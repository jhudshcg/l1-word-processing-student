0🚀 First steps: make, save and reopen

💡 Why this helps

Finding your saved file is a skill you will use in every unit.

Start with a small practice document. Your project comes next.

  1. Open desktop Word and choose Blank document.
  2. Type a title and three sentences about something you enjoy.
  3. Select a word and change it. Use Ctrl+Z to undo, then Ctrl+Y to redo.
  4. Click in your title. Choose Home→Styles→Heading 1.
  5. Choose File→Save As. In your college OneDrive, create a Word Processing folder. Save as Firstname-Surname-Practice.docx.
  6. Close the document. Open your folder and double-click the file to reopen it.

🛠️ Try it

Add a fourth sentence, save, close and reopen again.

🔎 Check before moving on

Is your new sentence still there? Show your teacher. Ask for practice on any step that felt tricky.

1🎨 Explore templates and choose your topic

💡 Why this helps

A template gives you a starting layout. Your choices make it your own.

A template is a document with a ready-made layout. Try the samples below in copies; their sample text is for you to replace.

  • 🏡Interior design sample
  • 📝Student report sample
  • 🦕3D student report sample
  1. Look at all three. Save a copy of one in your own folder before editing.
  2. Change the title. Click in a heading and look at its style in Home→Styles. Try changing the style.
  3. Open File→New and browse the templates available. Decide which could suit your guide, or start blank.
  4. Choose your interest and reader. Download the topic planning form. Save a copy, then click its fields to fill in your details and Section 1.

Leave the rest of the form until later tasks. In the booklet, name two templates and say when each would be useful. You do not need an essay.

📋 Plan your pages

Build at least five pages: a cover, automatic contents and three short content pages. Here is one way to organise your guide:

  • Page 1 — Cover: your title, subtitle, name and a suitable image.
  • Page 2 — Contents: an automatic list of your headings and page numbers.
  • Page 3 — Getting started: introduce your topic with a short explanation and picture.
  • Page 4 — Useful facts: compare information in your table and add a tip box.
  • Page 5 — Next steps: share useful advice and a link to find out more.

Choose content-page headings that suit your topic. Pictures, lists and tables can share the pages with short paragraphs.

🛠️ Try it

Choose your starting template. Save your guide as Firstname-Surname-InterestGuide.docx. Replace its sample title and remove unwanted sample text.

🔎 Check before moving on

Can you explain why your template or blank page suits your reader? If a form field will not work, ask your teacher for help opening it in desktop Word.

2🖼️ Add text, pictures and a link

💡 Why this helps

Text and pictures can work together to explain your topic.

  1. Find useful information from at least two sources overall. Write a short paragraph in your own words.
  2. Choose two pictures. Use your own or check permission to reuse them. Record sources and any required credit in Booklet Task 2. Ask your teacher when unsure.
  3. Choose Insert→Pictures→This Device to add a saved picture. Resize using a corner handle.
  4. Start with Picture Format→Wrap Text→In Line with Text for a predictable position. Try other wrapping later if useful.
  5. Right-click a picture and find View/Edit Alt Text. Briefly describe the information it adds for someone who cannot see it.
  6. Type useful link words, such as “Read the game rules”. Select them, press Ctrl+K, paste the web address and confirm.

Complete Section 2 of your planning form as you gather content. Refer to your booklet log instead of copying source details twice.

🛠️ Try it

Add a short paragraph, two pictures and one link to your own guide.

🔎 Check before moving on

Does the link open the right page? Are the images clear and unstretched? Can you find their sources again?

3🧭 Organise headings and automatic contents

💡 Why this helps

Heading styles help readers find their way and build your contents automatically.

Heading 1 is a main section. Heading 2 is a smaller section inside it. For example: “Getting started” and “What you need”. Two heading levels are enough.

Two levels are enough

A main heading introduces a section. A smaller heading divides it into useful parts.

Getting startedHeading 1 · main section

What you needHeading 2 · smaller section

  1. Apply Title and Subtitle to your cover text using Home→Styles.
  2. Click in each main heading and apply Heading 1. Apply Heading 2 to at least one smaller heading.
  3. Right-click Heading 1 in the Styles gallery and choose Modify. Change its colour or size and confirm. This updates text using that style.
  4. Use Ctrl+Enter after the cover to start a new page. Reserve page 2 for contents; use another page break before your main text.
  5. Click on page 2. Choose References→Table of Contents→Automatic Table. Use the automatic title provided.
  6. After changes, click the contents and choose Update Table→Update entire table.

Complete Section 3 of your planning form. Its Heading 3 choices are optional. Build at least five pages: a cover, contents and three short content pages, using pictures, lists and tables alongside text.

🛠️ Try it

Apply both heading levels, modify one style and insert your contents.

🔎 Check before moving on

Change one heading, then update the contents. Does it show the new wording? If a heading is missing, check its style.

4✍️ Format paragraphs, a list and a text box

💡 Why this helps

A clear layout helps your reader notice the useful information.

  1. Right-click Normal in Home→Styles and choose Modify.
  2. Choose a readable font and size: 12pt or larger if needed. In Format→Paragraph, try 1.15 or 1.5 line spacing and 6pt space after. Confirm the changes.
  3. Select your body paragraphs and apply Normal. Leave headings in their heading styles.
  4. Select a few short items and click Home→Bullets or Numbering. Use numbers when order matters.
  5. Choose Insert→Text Box→Simple Text Box. Replace the sample text with your own useful tip.
  6. Select the box border. Use Shape Format→Shape Fill / Shape Outline to choose clear colours. Keep the text readable against its background.

Complete Section 4 of your planning form now. Choose Other / Custom if you need a larger font than its listed options.

🛠️ Try it

Make a list and a tip box. Change Normal style to improve paragraph spacing.

🔎 Check before moving on

Does your list need numbers or bullets? Explain your choice to a partner. Check that the text box does not cover other text.

5📊 Create and change a table

💡 Why this helps

A table makes related facts easier to compare.

  1. Click where the table should go. Choose Insert→Table. Start with three columns and three rows.
  2. Use the top row for labels, such as Item / Fact / Tip. Fill in two rows below.
  3. Right-click in a cell. Choose Insert→Insert Rows Below. Fill your new row.
  4. Drag a column boundary to give longer text more room.
  5. Choose a clear style in Table Design. Check the heading row stands out.
  6. Select the whole table. Choose Table Design→Borders→All Borders. Check the borders are visible in print preview.

🛠️ Try it

Create a table for your topic, add a row or column, and record that change in Booklet Task 5.

🔎 Check before moving on

Can a reader follow the information across each row? Is any text squeezed into a very narrow column?

6📄 Set up pages and check the preview

💡 Why this helps

Print preview lets you spot page problems before handing in your guide.

  1. Choose Layout→Size→A4, then suitable Orientation and Margins. Portrait and Normal margins are a useful starting point.
  2. Use Ctrl+Enter for a new page, rather than pressing Enter repeatedly.
  3. Choose Insert→Page Number→Bottom of Page and a simple number style. Close Header and Footer.
  4. Open File→Print to inspect the preview. You do not need to print.
  5. Check every page. Fix cut-off text, unwanted blank pages and pictures covering text. Use Home→¶ to reveal extra paragraph marks if needed.

Simple page numbers are enough, including on the cover. Running headers, section breaks and Page X of Y are optional extensions.

🛠️ Try it

Check every page in preview and fix one layout problem if you find one.

🔎 Check before moving on

Can you see the whole table and text box? Is there an unexpected blank page?

7💬 Get feedback and make corrections

💡 Why this helps

A fresh pair of eyes can catch something you have missed.

  1. Open Review→Editor or Spelling & Grammar. Read each suggestion before accepting it; names may already be correct.
  2. Ask a partner: “What is clear? What should I improve?” They can use Review→New Comment, or tell you. Ask your teacher if no partner is available.
  3. Use Word tools to make two useful corrections. For repeated text, try Ctrl+H and check each replacement. Use cut and paste to move content if needed.
  4. In Booklet Task 7, write brief before/after notes and name the tools you used. Keep checking until your guide meets your reader's needs.

🛠️ Try it

Act on feedback and record two corrections. If you cannot find a problem, ask your teacher to review the guide with you.

🔎 Check before moving on

Has the correction improved the meaning or presentation? Do not accept every spelling suggestion automatically.

8💾 Save, retrieve and export

💡 Why this helps

The DOCX is your editable copy; the PDF is your finished copy to share.

  1. Save your guide in your own Word Processing folder as Firstname-Surname-InterestGuide.docx.
  2. Close it. Open that folder and reopen the saved file. Show your teacher, make a small edit and save again.
  3. Choose File→Export→Create PDF/XPS, or Save As→PDF. Use the same folder and file name.
  4. Open the PDF. Check every page and test the hyperlink. If something is wrong, fix the DOCX and export again.

Keep the DOCX so you can edit your work. Use the PDF to share the finished layout.

🛠️ Try it

Demonstrate reopening the correct saved guide and show both file types in your folder.

🔎 Check before moving on

Does the PDF contain your latest changes? Can you tell which file is editable?
